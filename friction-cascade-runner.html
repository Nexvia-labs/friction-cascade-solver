Friction Cascade Solver

Top-Down Cascade Method for stacked friction blocks, with one friction number per surface (Standard) or separate static and kinetic coefficients (Advanced). Runs on your phone; nothing is uploaded.

How μs ≠ μk is handled
  1. Test with μs. An interface yields only if the force it must supply is more than its static limit fs = μs·g·M. The same test decides whether an interface inside a sliding group fractures.
  2. Push with μk. Once an interface slips it exerts only fk = μk·g·M. That is the force handed down to the next group and the friction on the bottom of a sliding group.
  3. Re-join. If the final accelerations show a lower block running ahead of the block above it, that interface cannot be slipping the assumed way. It holds (or slips the other way) and the groups are solved again, until every interface obeys its friction law.

When μs > μk, Coulomb’s law alone can allow more than one consistent answer, for example a block that could either stay put or break loose. The method follows its fixed top-down order, and the Physical check confirms that the answer obeys Newton’s laws and both friction laws.
